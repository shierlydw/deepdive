Research

Design is the search for a good fit between a form, and it's context.

What makes a problem what it is: human needs, technical constraints, conflicting requirements, habits, edge cases, and relationships. Bad design appears where those forces remain unresolved. Good design appears where those misfits have been worked through carefully. Christopher Alexander, Notes on the Synthesis of Form (1964), via Karri Saarinen, 2026

In here, my aim is to first shape the problem. Before I can work on the form, I must know the fit.

A theory or hypothesis

User Preference

Insight

Method: Remote interview (audio)

Sample size: 2

Duration: 20-25 mins

A note on sample size

I opened the session for 3 days. Finding the right people, Malaysians who support family financially was hard, and money is a sensitive thing to ask my peers and strangers about. So I landed with depth over numbers: two of in-depth interview, treated as early, directional findings.

User Persona

HK

"Must not cause me to take additional steps from my current behavior, is there incentive for me to do that?"

Henry K (Mid 50s)

Behavior
  • Scheduled for baseline amount and ad-hoc for variables
  • Provides allowance to children on weekly basis
  • Manages expenses mainly through bank accounts, joint account with his spouse, and e-wallet for children
  • Won't change behaviour without a clear reason or incentive
Profile
  • Full time employee in private sector, steady salary income
  • 2 children studying in university dependent for academic support and a portion of living cost
  • 1 Older Dependent fixed amount support with occasional ad-hoc transfer
  • Shares financial responsibility with his spouse
Preference
  • "Keeping it secure doesn't necessarily translate to speed and automation, I value security above anything else"
  • He manages across a few channels and is fine with it, aims to not add on more fragmentation/new channels
Pain Points
  • Mild pain point, does not see major issues with services offered by banks and other financial services
  • Does not seek new features, only enhancements towards convenience, speed, and security
SJ

"Even though we give them the indication, they will not be able to remember"

Shibu J (Early 40s)

Behavior
  • Supports children on a needs basis, provides daily budget, supervised credit card, not a fixed allowance
  • Supports parents on fixed amount transferred, foreign currency exchange
  • Manages more than 10 facilities
Profile
  • Full time employee in private sector, expat working abroad
  • 2 young children in their primary school
  • Parents are Older Dependent fixed amount support with occasional ad-hoc transfer
  • Sole provider to his family
Preference
  • Uses e-wallets deliberately as a buffer, reduce digital footprints on low-risk facilities
  • Selects institutions on practical criteria, speed of transfer, convenience, exchange rate ranks 3rd
  • Sees himself as the steward of his growing wealth
Pain Points
  • Children accessed saved card details and made accidental in app purchases
  • Elderly parents struggle with digital banking, remembering password, prolonged login inactivity, surprise minimum-balance charges

Executive Summary

Elderly parents' digital banking struggles

From forgetting minimum balances to password confusion - digital tools fail elderly parents, creating ongoing friction in family financial support.

Security drives channel selection

Participants actively choose channels that limit exposure - eWallets as buffers, avoiding cash for elderly parents.

Multi-channel financial fragmentation

Participants manage numerous institutions simultaneously. Fragmentation is accepted as necessary - not a problem to solve.

High inertia without clear incentives

"It must not cause me additional steps." They won't switch without clear monetary incentives or convenience gains.

Dual transfer: scheduled + ad-hoc

Regular scheduled transfers combined with ad-hoc payments - a universal mental model for family money flows.

Gradual financial independence

Participants deliberately structure support to build independence - transitioning from joint to individual accounts over time.

Solution

Selected Solution

Trusted Contact Person (Safety Net)

As a proxy to help elderly, guard activities, or be reachable. A caretaker adult nominates a trusted contact to receive alerts about suspicious transactions on an elderly parent's account - oversight without removing autonomy.

Why I selected this solution

  • ·I chose the elderly segment, where the unmet gap is widest.
  • ·The bank has invested well in the young and digitally savvy. Youth savings accounts, allowance tools, shared savings pots, bill splitting, expense tracking. The day-to-day money management space is well served.
  • ·Malaysian banks do offer security features. Fund locking, account kill switches, transfer limits, ATM cash-out controls. These are useful, but they all assume one thing: that the account holder can act on their own. For an elderly user who forgets a password, misses a minimum balance, or gets talked into a transfer, self-protection is not enough.
  • ·None of these features involve a second, consented person. That is the gap.
